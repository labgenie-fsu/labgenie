Questions

About the registry

What is the LabGenie Volunteer Registry?

It is a list of people who have told us they would like to hear about research opportunities with the LabGenie project at Florida State University.

Joining means our research team can contact you when a study comes up that matches your interests and for which you may be eligible. You are not signing up for any particular study by joining.

Does joining the registry mean I am taking part in a research study?

No. Joining the registry and taking part in a study are two separate things.

Every study has its own consent process, which happens before you take part in anything. You will always be told what a study involves, how long it takes, and what compensation is offered, and you can say no.

Can I leave the registry later?

Yes, at any time and without giving a reason. Email us at zhe@fsu.edu and we will remove your details.

Leaving the registry does not affect anything else, and you are welcome to rejoin later if you change your mind.

Taking part

Who can take part?

Our current studies are primarily recruiting adults aged 60 and over. Some studies also include family caregivers, and occasionally other groups such as healthcare professionals.

Each study has its own criteria, so what makes someone a good fit varies from one study to the next. Joining the registry is the simplest way to find out when something suits you. If you are not sure whether you qualify, please join anyway and we will let you know.

What kinds of studies do you run?

Most of our studies ask for your opinions and feedback on health technology. That might mean answering survey questions, talking with a researcher about your experiences, or trying out a piece of software and telling us what is confusing about it.

These are not medical studies. We do not test treatments or medicines, and nobody will examine you or give you medical advice.

How long do studies usually take?

It depends on the study. Online surveys tend to take 10 to 20 minutes. Interviews, focus groups, and design workshops usually run between 30 and 90 minutes. Some longer studies involve more than one session over a period of weeks or months.

You will always be told how much time is involved before you decide whether to take part.

Where do studies take place?

Studies may take place online, over Zoom, by telephone, or in person, depending on what the study is looking at. You will be told which before you decide.

You can tell us on the registry form which of these you would be willing to do, and we will keep that in mind when matching you to studies.

Do I have to take part in every study I am invited to?

No. You can say no to any invitation, as many times as you like, and you do not need to give a reason.

Declining will not affect whether we contact you about future studies, and it will not affect your care or your relationship with any healthcare provider.

Compensation

Will I be compensated?

Many of our studies provide compensation for taking part. The amount varies from study to study, depending on how much time is involved.

Whenever we invite you to a study, we will tell you what compensation is offered before you decide whether to take part.

How is compensation provided?

Compensation is usually provided as a gift card. The details for a particular study, including how and when it is sent, will be explained when we contact you about that study.

There is no cost to you at any point, and joining the registry itself is free.

Your information

How will my information be used?

The details you give us on the registry form are used for two things: to contact you about research opportunities, and to work out which studies you might be eligible for.

Your information is never used for marketing and is never sold.

Who will have access to my information?

The LabGenie research team at Florida State University. Registry information is held securely in university systems and access is limited to team members working on recruitment for these studies.

Will my information be shared outside the research team?

No, not without your permission. The only exceptions are the routine oversight that applies to all university research, such as review by the Florida State University Institutional Review Board, and anything we are required to do by law.

Keeping in touch

How often will you contact me?

Only when there is a research opportunity we think may suit you. There is no newsletter and no regular mailing, so for most people this means occasional contact rather than anything frequent.

If you would prefer to hear from us less often, or not at all, just let us know.

How can I update my information?

Email zhe@fsu.edu with whatever has changed, and we will update your details. This includes a change of phone number, email address, or how you would prefer us to get in touch.

Who should I contact if I have questions?

Dr. Zhe He leads the project and is the best first point of contact.

Email zhe@fsu.edu. We are happy to answer questions before you decide whether to join.
