What's involved

One hour, from your own kitchen table.

You'll meet one of our researchers on a video call and try the tool while telling us what you think of it.

There's nothing to prepare and nothing to study. You'll look at a sample set of lab results, poke around, and say out loud what makes sense and what doesn't. If something confuses you, that's the most useful thing you can tell us, because it means we have something to fix.

Who can join

You can take part if…

  • You are 65 or older
  • You live with two or more of these conditions:
    • Hypothyroidism
    • Hyperthyroidism
    • Diabetes (Type 1 or 2)
    • Chronic kidney disease
    • High blood pressure
    • High cholesterol
    • Anemia
    • Heart disease
  • You've had at least one lab test in the past six months or so
  • You have a laptop, desktop, or tablet with a working camera and microphone, and can join a one-hour video call
  • You speak English

This study isn't a fit if…

  • You currently work in healthcare, because we're looking for the patient's point of view
  • You're already taking part in another study about health technology

The session

What actually happens in the hour

  1. We say hello and explain everything

    We go over what we're doing and answer your questions before anything starts. With your permission we record the session so we don't miss anything. It is used only by our research team and is never shared publicly.

  2. A short warm-up

    We'll ask you to open a website you use often and talk through what you're looking at. It's practice for the main part, where we ask you to think out loud.

  3. You step into someone else's shoes

    We'll ask you to imagine you're a patient named James or Mary, who has a thyroid condition and has just had lab work done. The results are made up for this study. You'll never be asked about your own results.

  4. You try the tool and say what you think

    You'll look around the dashboard, find the results, try the chat assistant, and look up past tests, narrating as you go. We stay mostly quiet. There are no right answers and nothing is graded.

  5. A few questions to finish

    A short questionnaire and a conversation about what worked, what didn't, and the one thing you'd change. Then we're done.

The project

Why we're building LabGenie

Viewing lab results is one of the most-used features of online patient portals. It's also one of the most confusing.

Portals give people direct access to their test results, doctors' notes, and medication lists. That access is valuable, and the people with the most to gain are older adults managing several conditions at once. But results usually arrive as clinical shorthand: reference ranges, flags, and abbreviations, with little explanation. Older adults with limited health literacy or less experience with technology run into the most trouble making sense of them.

LabGenie is a web-based tool built to close that gap. It shows results visually, explains them in plain language, and suggests questions tailored to a person's own health information, so they arrive at an appointment prepared rather than puzzled.

The project is led by researchers at Florida State University and built through a user-centered process. That's what these sessions are: nothing gets finalized without feedback from the people who'll actually use it.

Questions

Questions people ask

Do I need to be good with computers?

No. If computers aren't your strong suit, your feedback is especially valuable, because we can't learn where a tool is confusing from people who never get confused. You do need a computer or tablet with a camera and microphone, and we'll help you get onto the video call.

Will I have to share my own medical records?

No. The session uses made-up lab results for a fictional patient. You will never be asked to bring, upload, or discuss your own test results, and nothing you see should be used to make decisions about your own health.

Is this a medical appointment? Will I get medical advice?

No. This is a research session about the design of a tool. Nobody will examine you, review your health, or give you medical advice. LabGenie is not a substitute for talking with your doctor.

Why do you need my camera on?

Seeing your face helps us understand where you're hesitating or looking puzzled, which is exactly the information we need to improve the design. With your permission we record the session for the research team's use. It is never shared publicly.

What happens to my information if I sign up?

Your contact details are used by the research team to reach you about this study and, if you agree, about future studies you might be interested in. They are stored securely and are never sold or used for marketing. You can ask us to remove your information at any time.

Am I committing to anything by signing up?

No. Signing up means we may contact you about scheduling. You can decline, change your mind, or stop partway through a session at any point, for any reason, without giving an explanation.

I took part last year. Can I do it again?

Please get in touch and we'll let you know. Sessions are small and we try to hear from a mix of people across rounds, so it depends on where we are in the schedule.
