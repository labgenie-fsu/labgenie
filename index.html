About

What is LabGenie?

LabGenie is a research project at Florida State University developing and evaluating technologies that help older adults better understand laboratory test results and other health information.

Our research explores how artificial intelligence, better information design, and patient-centred technology can help people:

  • Better understand their health information
  • Prepare for conversations with healthcare providers
  • Feel more confident managing chronic health conditions
  • Become more active partners in their healthcare

Every technology we develop is informed by feedback from older adults, family caregivers, healthcare professionals, and community members.

Why join

Why join the Volunteer Registry?

Joining the registry allows our research team to contact you when future research opportunities match your interests and eligibility.

Every study is different. By joining the registry now, you can hear about new opportunities without having to go looking for them yourself.

Taking part is always voluntary.

What happens next

What happens after I join?

  1. Complete the two-minute Volunteer Registry form.

  2. We review your information and identify studies that match your interests.

  3. If you appear eligible for a study, we contact you with more detail, including what taking part involves, the expected time commitment, and any compensation.

  4. You decide whether or not you would like to take part.

Joining the registry never obliges you to take part in a study.

Quick questions

Questions people ask

Do I have to take part if I join?

No. Joining simply allows us to contact you about future research opportunities.

Will I be compensated?

Many studies provide compensation. It varies by study and will always be explained before you decide whether to take part.

Who can join?

Current studies are primarily recruiting adults aged 60 and over. Some studies also include family caregivers or other groups.

What happens after I join?

If a future study matches your interests, a member of our research team will contact you with more information.
